# Webinar slides: devil's-advocate review

Review of `docs/webinar-slides.md` (29 slides, ~58 min), written 30 Sep 2026. The goal it checks against: position Robogebra as useful for maths teaching, not sell it.

## Duplicate and overlapping challenges

1. **Challenges 3, 4 and 5 are one challenge ("tech adoption is hard") told three ways.**
   - Challenge 3: tools don't fit in 40 minutes.
   - Challenge 4: learning the tool takes too long.
   - Challenge 5: stuck between chalk and AI.

   Their easier ways repeat too. Slide 19 says "pick tools that follow your syllabus", Slide 20 says "pick tools with a low learning cost", and Slide 21 says "check: nothing new to learn, works with your board". The audience hears the same advice three times in a row.

2. **Challenges 7 and 9 are both "hunting through guidebooks for questions".** Practice variations and exam papers are different tasks, but the image is the same desk full of guides. Easier ways 7 (generate variations) and 9 (search past questions) are also close.

3. **A third of the list is about tools, not maths teaching.** Challenges 3, 4, 5 and 6 are problems with technology. A teacher who uses no tech scores all four 0. That makes the list look reverse-engineered from product features.

## Where it tips into selling

4. **Robogebra appears in 8 of the 10 easier ways.** Only Slides 18 (prompts) and 19 are tool-neutral. That's a drift from the agreed "best answer wins" rule.

5. **The "do it yourself with AI" option is a weak alternative.** It asks teachers to build a website (Slide 22), scan books into a searchable database (Slide 25), or build a student-tracking platform (Slide 26). Busy teachers won't. They'll see the option exists to make Robogebra the only real choice, and that's worse than naming Robogebra openly.

6. **We wrote the scoring rules and then gave Robogebra top marks.** The learning cost scale (Slide 20) is followed straight away by "Robogebra sits at the low end". The three-point check (Slide 21) ends with "That's how we built Robogebra." That's a vendor writing the rubric and then passing it.

7. **No easier way works without technology.** Every answer is a tool or AI. The only no-tech advice is coloured chalk, in one line of Slide 17's notes. Expect the question: "So your solution to everything is software?"

8. **The scoring always leads to the same place.** All three score ranges say the easier ways will help. No range says "you're doing fine". It's mild, but it's a pitch.

9. **Ashraf is the panelist and also the founder.** The Q&A can slide into product support ("Does Robogebra have Maharashtra board Class 11?"). Plan to steer those questions to The Vertex.

## Things that could go wrong on the day

- **Overclaiming.** "Any question in your textbook" (Slide 24) and unconfirmed board coverage for previous-year questions (Slide 25). One teacher disproving a claim in the chat damages every other slide.
- **The Indian context.**
  - Slides 21 and 22 assume a smart board.
  - Slide 26 assumes every student has a device.
  - Some schools restrict AI (Slide 18).

  Government and rural teachers may feel the session isn't for them.
- **Timing.** 1.5 minutes for each easier way, including a GIF. Every overrun comes out of Ashraf's Q&A.
- **Delivery.** The prompt library and the web-page playbook are promised publicly for "the coming week". If they're late, The Vertex starts with a broken promise.
- **Advice that could backfire.** Slide 18 implies a good prompt gives a correct answer on the first try. It should also tell teachers to check the answer.

## Recommended changes

1. **Merge challenges 3 and 4** into one moment covering setup time and learning time. That frees one slot.
2. **Refill the freed slot, and possibly challenge 7's, with non-tool pain points.** Options include a mixed-ability class, maths anxiety, and revision before the board exams. They're real, and they have no-tech easier ways.
3. **Give every easier way three levels:**
   - **Tomorrow, with chalk:** a no-tech move for the next period.
   - **With a free tool or AI:** something a busy teacher can realistically do.
   - **With Robogebra:** only where it really is the easiest.
4. **Show Robogebra in 4 or 5 demos, not 8.** The strongest are the step-by-step lesson (Slide 22), 3D step by step (Slide 24), Edit and Explore (Slide 23), and student journeys (Slide 26). Mention it once elsewhere, or leave it out.
5. **Add one honest line**, for example "Robogebra doesn't solve this one. Here's what works."
6. **Separate the rubric from the product.** Present the learning cost scale and the three-point check as neutral tools, and let teachers apply them to Robogebra themselves.
7. **Tighten the claims.** Say "questions like this one" instead of "any question", name the boards covered, and add "check the answer" on Slide 18.

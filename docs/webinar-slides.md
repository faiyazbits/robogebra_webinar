## Slide 17 — Easier way 1/10: Let colour connect the maths

- **On-screen text:**
  - Easier way 1 · for "Maths online looks lifeless"
  - Give each idea its own colour, and keep that colour everywhere it appears.
  - In y = **m**x + **c**, if m is blue in the equation, the slope is blue on the graph. If c is orange, the point where the line meets the y-axis is orange too. Students follow the colour and see where each part comes from.
- **Visual idea:** A GIF or screen recording of a Robogebra lesson:
  1. The equation y = 2x + 3 appears, with **2** in blue and **3** in orange.
  2. The line is graphed. The rise-over-run triangle showing the slope is drawn in blue, and the y-intercept (0, 3) is marked with an orange dot.
  3. The blue 2 changes to 3 and the line gets steeper, with the blue triangle growing alongside it.

  Put it next to a small, faded thumbnail of the grey plain-text version from Slide 4 so the change is obvious. Overlay pill: "Same maths, now in colour"
- **Speaker notes:** Remember those grey pages from the first moment? Here's the easier way: colour. When each part of an idea has its own colour, and keeps it everywhere, students stop hunting for connections because the colour shows them. Look at this Robogebra lesson. In y equals 2x plus 3, the 2 is blue and the 3 is orange. On the graph, the slope triangle is blue and the point where the line crosses the y-axis is orange. A student doesn't need you to say "the m value gives the slope" three times, because they can see it. Now watch what happens when I change the 2 to a 3. The blue triangle grows and the line gets steeper. The colour carries the link from symbol to picture. You can do this with coloured chalk tomorrow, and a tool like Robogebra keeps the colours matched for you on every line and every graph.

## Slide 19 — Easier way 3/10: Get the tool ready before the bell

- **On-screen text:**
  - Easier way 3 · for "Brilliant tools, 40-minute periods"
  - Keep the tool's setup out of your period.
  - **Pick tools that follow your syllabus.** When a tool is organised by your chapters, the right screen is one tap away.
  - **Prepare it the night before.** If your tool isn't organised that way, set up the exact screen you need, save it, and keep it ready to open on the smart board.
  - **Dry-run it three times.** Walk through the lesson on the tool at least three times before you use it with a class.
- **Visual idea:** Two timelines of the same 40-minute period, one above the other.
  - Top (faded): "Open laptop, find tool, search for the right screen, adjust sliders" takes up the first 10 minutes, and the bell rings mid-demo.
  - Bottom (bright): "Tap saved lesson" takes 30 seconds, and the rest of the period is teaching and student questions.

  A small checklist card on the side: "✓ Follows my chapter ✓ Saved and ready ✓ Dry-run ×3". Overlay pill on the bottom timeline: "Ready in 30 seconds"
- **Speaker notes:** Those brilliant tools can fit into a forty-minute period. The trick is to keep their setup out of the period. First, where you can, use tools that follow your syllabus chapter by chapter. Then the screen for today's lesson is already there, and you don't have to build it in front of forty students. Second, if the tool you love doesn't work that way, do the setup the night before. Build the exact screen you need, save it, and keep it one tap away on the smart board, so in class you just open it and teach. And third, a lesson from experience: never try a tool for the first time in front of a live class. Run through the lesson on it at least three times on your own. The first run shows you where things go wrong, and by the third you know exactly which button to press while you're still talking to the class. That's how the tool gets into your period, and how your syllabus stays on track.
